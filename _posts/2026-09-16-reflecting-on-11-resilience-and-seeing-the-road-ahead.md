Life has a way of serving up milestones and curveballs in the exact same week.

We recently celebrated my son's 11th birthday, and sitting there watching him blow out his candles, I couldn't help but feel a deep sense of gratitude. Looking back at how much he's grown, developed, and matured over the past few years fills me with pride. He's becoming his own person—curious, capable, and sharp. Of course, no 11-year-old is a finished product. There's still plenty of room to grow in areas like kindness, patience, and graciousness toward others. But watching that evolution unfold is easily one of the most rewarding parts of parenthood.

On the other home front, life threw us a reminder of how quickly things can change. My wife has been dedicated to improving her health and getting into a strong exercise routine recently. Just as she was building momentum, she suffered a bad fall during a workout and ended up fracturing her arm. It was a frustrating setback, to say the least. But such is life—it rarely asks for permission before disrupting our plans. The real test isn't avoiding the fall; it's refusing to let a temporary setback hold you back. Adapt, heal, and keep moving forward.

Which naturally brings me to the next question: What's next for me?

Whenever a family member hits a bump in the road, it forces you to look in the mirror and audit your own health. Overall, I feel fine, but I've been increasingly worried about my eyes lately. The floaters have been getting more noticeable and frequent, and it's a jarring reminder that my body isn't invincible. Eye health isn't something to take for granted, and I need to start prioritizing protection, regular check-ups, and proper care before minor annoyances become bigger issues.

Taking stock of all this—my son growing up fast, my wife healing up, and my own health reminders—serves as a good reset button. Growth isn't linear for any of us. Whether it's teaching an 11-year-old to navigate the world with a bit more grace, supporting my wife through her recovery, or taking proactive steps to safeguard my own well-being, the mission remains the same: keep reflecting, keep protecting what matters, and step into whatever comes next with clear eyes.
